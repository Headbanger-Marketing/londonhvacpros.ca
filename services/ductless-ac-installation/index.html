Ductless AC Installation

Which rooms need separate control, and should the system provide cooling only or heating as well?

A ductless mini-split installation begins with the rooms to be served and how those spaces are used. The number and placement of indoor units affect zoning, while the outdoor-unit location and line-set route shape the installation work. A cooling-only model and a model intended for heating as well as cooling serve different purposes, so equipment selection should reflect the requested operation. Where a central system already serves some rooms, that arrangement also informs which spaces need separate control.

The assessment should identify the rooms to serve, their layout, and the intended heating or cooling use. Indoor-unit locations, outdoor-unit placement, and the route for the refrigerant line set affect installation details and room coverage. If heating is required, model capabilities and existing heat sources matter; a cooling-only unit is not a substitute for a heating-capable system.

Assessment and Scope

Questions for the Proposed Work

01

Plan zones around room use

A zone is defined by the space served by an indoor unit and the control arrangement for that unit. Planning should consider which rooms are occupied separately and whether doors or the layout limit air movement between them. Those observations influence the number and location of indoor units. Treating every floor or room as one zone can leave the controls poorly matched to how the space is actually used.

02

Choose equipment for the intended operation

Cooling-only equipment and heating-and-cooling models are not interchangeable choices. If the request includes heating, the selected model's stated operating capabilities and the property's existing heat source should be reviewed together. Equipment ratings and model details help define what the system is designed to do; they do not by themselves guarantee a particular result in every room or season.

03

Coordinate placement and line-set routing

Installation planning includes the indoor mounting position, outdoor-unit location, and the route between them for the line set. The route and required wall opening affect where equipment can be placed and what installation work is involved. Where an existing opening is being reused, its location and condition should be checked rather than assumed suitable. Electrical and manufacturer requirements also inform the final equipment arrangement.

Compare the next step

Coordinate the head positions and routes with framing, electrical rough-in, insulation, and finishes. Ask which penetrations and drain details must be decided early and which service points remain exposed. The completed scope should include testing and occupant instructions after the room is ready for its intended use.
